## Overview

This case study examines how **JPMorgan Chase** uses machine learning, large language models (LLMs), entity extraction, and behavioral analytics to detect **business email compromise (BEC)** and other forms of financial fraud.

## Problem

Financial fraud has grown more sophisticated as criminals use social engineering, fake vendor instructions, compromised email accounts, and AI-generated messages. Traditional rule-based systems struggle to keep pace with evolving tactics. Financial institutions need AI systems that can analyze patterns across emails, payment instructions, account behavior, and sanctions data at scale.

## What I Built

A comprehensive case study report analyzing JPMorgan Chase's layered AI fraud-detection strategy:

- **Technology analysis:** Machine learning models, LLMs for text analysis, entity extraction from unstructured data, behavioral analytics, and risk-scoring pipelines
- **Process mapping:** How data flows from emails and transactions through AI models to human-reviewed alerts
- **Benefits assessment:** Speed, contextual understanding, multi-channel detection, reduced false positives
- **Challenges review:** Data quality, model explainability, adversarial AI, privacy, operational requirements
- **Recommendations:** Layered fraud strategy, data governance, human-in-the-loop, fairness monitoring

## Key Findings

- AI does not replace fraud professionals — it amplifies their ability to detect patterns at scale
- LLMs are particularly valuable for BEC because fraud often begins with language (fake emails, payment instructions)
- The strongest fraud programs combine AI with validated data, human review, governance, and continuous improvement
- Adversarial AI creates an ongoing arms race requiring constant investment
